       Frequency Domain System Identiﬁcation
               Using Arbitrary Signals

         R. Pintelon, J. Schoukens, and G. Vandersteen


  Abstract—It is the common conviction that frequency domain system
identiﬁcation suffers from the drawback that it cannot handle arbitrary
signals without introducing systematic errors. This paper shows that it
is possible to deal with nonperiodic signals without any approximation
and under the same assumptions as in the time domain, by estimating
simultaneously some initial conditions and the system model parameters.
  Index Terms— Frequency domain identiﬁcation, parametric transfer
function models.

                                                                                                                                  (a)
                                    I. INTRODUCTION
  Until now frequency domain system identiﬁcation assumes that
the input and output signals are periodic or time limited within the
observation time [1]–[4]. This is necessary to guarantee a leakage-free
calculation of the input and output signal spectra through the discrete
Fourier transform [5]. Indeed, suppose that the nonperiodic input u(t)
and output y(t) signals (see Fig. 1) of a discrete-time system G(z  1)
are observed without any errors (no measurement and no process
noise) at time instants t = 0; Ts; 2Ts;      ; (N   1)Ts; where Ts
is the sampling time. The problem with these signals in frequency
domain identiﬁcation is that the ratio of the corresponding discrete
Fourier spectra is not equal to the true transfer function (see Fig. 2)
